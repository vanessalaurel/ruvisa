## III. Methodology

### A. Datasets and Preprocessing

Ruvisa uses four data sources spanning the modalities it fuses—facial images for skin analysis and a scraped commercial catalogue providing products, reviews, and ingredient knowledge—all projected onto the unified seven-concern taxonomy (acne, comedonal acne, pigmentation, acne scars/texture, pores, redness, wrinkles) that keeps the pipelines mutually comparable.

**Facial image data.** Lesion localization is trained on the Roboflow Acne Detection dataset (v2) [Roboflow] (~8,000 annotated images over six classes—acne, redness, comedonal acne, pigmentation, acne scars, flat wart), with semantically equivalent classes (e.g., pustule, papular) merged into a unified *acne* class, an 80/10/10 split, and 1280×1280 resizing to preserve small-lesion detail. Lesion severity is trained on ACNE04 [ACNE04], whose crops carry four ordinal levels (none/mild/moderate/severe) grounded in the Hayashi grading criterion [Hayashi]; we use the dataset's five predefined CV folds with 90/10 stratified train/validation splitting and map the four levels to evenly spaced scores in [0,1] for downstream aggregation. The wrinkle branch uses FFHQ [FFHQ] (1,000 of 70,000 images carry manual binary wrinkle masks) with the two-stage weak-supervision strategy of Moon et al. [Moon2024]—Stage 1 pretrains on Gaussian high-pass texture maps (masked to face-skin regions) as weak labels, Stage 2 fine-tunes on the manual masks; we reuse the released Stage-2 U-Net weights. Detector training uses YOLOv8's built-in augmentation, and the severity classifier uses standard crop/flip/color-jitter/blur augmentations with ImageNet normalization.

**Product, review, and ingredient data.** The commercial corpus is scraped from Sephora Hong Kong [Sephora], yielding 1,099 products (metadata, INCI lists, ratings, reviews), of which 1,009 carry valid ingredient lists, plus 5,965 reviews over 872 products (about 6.8 reviews per product on average; the distribution is strongly right-skewed, so products with few reviews fall back to ingredient evidence). Formulation is interpreted through the standardized INCI naming system mandated internationally (EU EC 1223/2009; U.S. 21 CFR Part 701) [INCI-EU], [INCI-US]. To turn ingredients into concern evidence, we scrape INCIDecoder [INCIDecoder] over seven primary function pages (anti-acne, skin-brightening, soothing, exfoliant, cell-communicating-ingredient, astringent, antioxidant) and three supplementary pages (surfactant-cleansing, abrasive-scrub, moisturizer-humectant). This produces two artifacts: one mapping each ingredient to its INCIDecoder functions, and one mapping each ingredient to the seven concerns after projecting every function onto the taxonomy. The merged lookup of 3,118 unique ingredients supplies the position-aware evidence behind each product's concern vector.
